## The problem

Harborline's dispatchers were running 140 trucks across three states from a spreadsheet, two radios and a whiteboard. When a truck ran late, finding a replacement meant calling drivers one by one. The average reassignment took six minutes, and on a bad morning the whole board fell behind.

## What we built

A live dispatch board that shows every truck, load and driver on one screen, updated in real time. The core idea was simple: **put the next decision in front of the dispatcher before they go looking for it.**

- Late trucks rise to the top with the three best replacements already ranked.
- One keystroke reassigns a load and texts the driver.
- Every change is undoable for thirty seconds, so nobody hesitates.

> The first week, a dispatcher told us she'd stopped keeping her paper backup. That was the moment we knew it worked.

## Decisions worth sharing

### Keyboard first, mouse welcome

Dispatchers live on this screen for ten hours. We designed every action for the keyboard first, then made sure it still felt natural with a mouse. A command palette covers anything without a shortcut.

### Optimistic updates with honest failure

Changes apply instantly on screen and sync in the background. If the server rejects one, the row turns amber and explains why in plain words, rather than silently snapping back.

```ts
const result = await reassign(load, truck);
if (!result.ok) flagRow(load.id, result.reason);
```

## Results

1. Median reassignment time fell from six minutes to 48 seconds.
2. On-time delivery rose from 86 to 94 percent over the first quarter.
3. Two dispatchers now cover a shift that used to need three, with less overtime.
